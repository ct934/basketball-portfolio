Scouting

Stephon Castle

Scouting report workspace for notes, evaluation context, and final draft outlook.

Stats

PTS: 10.9 | REB: 4.5 | AST: 3.0 | STL: 0.9 | BLK 0.6 | FG%: 47.1 | 3PT%: 28.8

Info

School: UConn | Class: Freshman | Position: CG | Height: 6'6" | Weight: 215 | Wingspan: 6'9"

NBA Comparison:

Floor: Bruce Brown
Ceiling: Shai Gilgeous-Alexander
Archetype: Two-Way Lead Guard

Offense

While playing alongside two of the Big East's top 10 assist leaders in Tristen Newton and Cam Spencer, Stephon Castle still averaged 3.0 assists per game as a freshman, ranking 18th in the conference. That experience positioned him well as a connective passer, an ideal complement to a team like the Spurs, where Victor Wembanyama is expected to take on a significant playmaking role. Castle thrived in that secondary role at UConn, often creating offense by pushing in transition or penetrating zones to find bigs inside. With Wembanyama commanding attention and space, those reads and assists should come even easier at the next level.

Defense

Castle’s defensive impact was one of the cornerstones of his freshman season at UConn. Despite averaging just 27 minutes per game, he posted 0.8 steals and 0.5 blocks, using his 6’6” frame, 6’9” wingspan, and elite athleticism to guard multiple positions and disrupt opposing offenses. His instincts, active hands, and willingness to challenge shots at the rim translated into strong advanced metrics, including a 1.7 Defensive Win Share and a 3.1 Defensive Box Plus-Minus.

Outlook

In this class, Castle may offer the highest floor and arguably the second-highest ceiling behind Alex Sarr. Despite playing a more limited offensive role at UConn, he was a key contributor throughout their national championship run, showcasing his willingness to buy into a team-first approach and adapt his game to whatever role is needed. That winning mentality and versatility align seamlessly with the culture in San Antonio under Coach Popovich. Castle’s ceiling projects as a Shai Gilgeous-Alexander-type guard, with composed, shifty ball-handling, creative finishing, and a tall frame that allows him to score over defenders and defend multiple positions. His floor, meanwhile, resembles a more skilled version of Bruce Brown, a high-level role player who thrives as a connective piece on a championship-caliber team.

Fit with the Spurs:

Stephon Castle is a strong fit for the Spurs and could emerge as the next cornerstone guard under Gregg Popovich. At UConn, Castle consistently showcased his ability to time cuts behind the defense, creating easy looks at the rim. This skill would be amplified playing alongside Victor Wembanyama. With Wembanyama often drawing multiple defenders and initiating offense from the elbow, Castle's movement off the ball, whether from the dunker spot or the weak side corner, could neutralize smaller help defenders. His instincts and activity in these off-ball actions also align well with Popovich’s motion-heavy system, which emphasizes cutting, spacing, and fluid ball movement. One of Castle’s most intriguing off-ball qualities is his effectiveness as a roll man in pick-and-roll situations. His timing, touch, and willingness to play physically in tight spaces give him versatility as more than just a traditional guard. Paired with Wembanyama’s ability to pass over the top and manipulate defenses through switches and mismatches, Castle’s ability to slip screens or dive hard to the rim adds another layer to their potential synergy. It’s yet another reason why his skill set profiles as such a natural fit alongside Wembanyama in San Antonio’s evolving offense. Castle also adds value in transition. His ability to grab defensive rebounds and push the ball up the floor led to quick-hit opportunities. This is an area where the Spurs struggled to capitalize on Wembanyama's ability to sprint the floor fully. That vision and pace could help unlock easier buckets for San Antonio’s young star. While Castle may need time to expand his offensive arsenal as a full-time point guard, his two-way versatility, court awareness, and ability to play off of elite talent make him a compelling option for the Spurs’ long-term backcourt.
Pairing a defensively versatile guard like Castle with a generational rim protector in Victor Wembanyama gives San Antonio a foundation to build one of the most imposing defensive tandems in the league for years to come.
